## What it is

PsiQuantum, Palo Alto, is the comparator for this Genesis slice: a
fusion-based photonic architecture that has reached the deepest rung of the
same DARPA ladder Quantum Motion is climbing. Where the silicon-spin thesis
says *the existing fab industry scales qubits*, the photonic thesis says *the
hard physics lives entirely in components a high-volume fab can stamp out* —
sources, waveguides, switches, detectors — and networking photons replaces
wiring spins.

## What it has actually demonstrated

- **Omega chipset (Nature, February 2025)** — a complete, manufacturable
  photonic component set benchmarked at GlobalFoundries in New York: 99.98%
  dual-rail state preparation and measurement, 99.22% two-qubit fusion
  fidelity, 99.72% chip-to-chip interconnect. All figures are conditional on
  photon detection — the paper does not price in system loss, which is the
  architecture's governing unknown.
- **Manufacturing integration** — millions of devices, thousands of wafers,
  and a claimed ~500,000 device-level measurements per month, in a
  high-volume commercial fab.
- **Evaluation depth** — the only non-Microsoft company through to US2QC's
  final phase (≡ QBI Stage C), after a year-long review by a ~50-expert
  DARPA team; followed by a $31.8M Stage C tasking (Sep 2025) and a $125M
  expanded agreement (Jul 2026).

## What has not been demonstrated

No independently measured *system-level* quantum performance exists in the
record. The DARPA progression measures design viability and evaluation
access, not demonstrated utility-scale hardware. Groundbreaking of the
Brisbane and Chicago Quantum Compute Centers was announced for 2025;
completion and commissioning status is unverified in this ledger.

## Why it is the comparator

PsiQuantum shares Quantum Motion's load-bearing dependency — industrial
foundry manufacture — while differing on nearly everything else: photons
versus spins, fusion networks versus gate arrays, a room-temperature optical
plane plus cryogenic detectors versus an mK CMOS stack. If foundry
manufacturability is the durable advantage both claim, the observatory can
watch two different wagers on the same substrate race.
