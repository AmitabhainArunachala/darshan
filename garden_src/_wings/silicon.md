# Seeing the Silicon

## Where you are

You are at the door of **Seeing the Silicon**, a new wing of this garden. Darshan is for seeing clearly after the feed. This wing applies that to the industry that makes the substrate of machine thinking: lithography, memory, packaging, power, cooling, and the capital that piles up behind each of them.

Technological importance is not economic capture. Economic capture is not business quality. Business quality is not investment attractiveness. The wing exists to keep those four from collapsing into one word.

The law is simple, and it does not bend: this is research and education, never advice. Every number is sourced and dated. UNKNOWN is better than invented precision. A prediction carries a date and a resolving source, or it is not a prediction. You should leave more able to check the writer, never captured by the writer.

## The reading desk

This is the organ the other series do not have. Sources placed by the rhythm they belong to, so you can keep seeing the silicon after you leave the page. A link is given only where this session opened the page; a name without a link is a source we kept and could not verify live today. Free sources first. The paid tier is named as paid.

A first week is enough to start: Miller's *Chip War* for the map; Nenni and McLellan's *Fabless* for the business split the rest of the industry assumes; then the daily Taiwan and Japan skim below. SemiAnalysis waits until you can draw the fab flow. It assumes you already know what a wafer is.

## How to read

So that reading becomes evidence, not atmosphere.

- **Pen in hand.** Every book or paper yields at least ten claims and five questions. A reading that yields none was not read.
- **Write first.** For any question of mechanism or judgement, write your answer, timed, from memory. Then ask a model. Then compare. If you asked before writing, that session is over.
- **No number without a primary source you opened.** A model's number is a lead. A press release corroborates only itself.
- **Prepared remarks are marketing.** Read the Q&A. The 20-F is the document written under legal liability. The keynote is not.
- **Measured or inferred?** Ask it of every chart, every booth, every post. Prefer instruments that measure over voices that announce.
- **One dated prediction a week**, with a resolution date and a resolving source, or it is not a prediction.
- **One recorded mistake a week.** If there is none, the weekly review asks why.

This wing is research, never a recommendation. Companies are named for analysis. No ticker, no price, no recommendation belongs on these pages.

The visible winner is not the system. You can watch the accelerator and miss the substrate it sits on, the focus ring that sets the plasma at the wafer's edge, the ABF that ran short in 2021 and glut in 2023, the hybrid bonder that decides whether a stack is a product, the transformer with a two-to-four-year lead time. Attention in this industry is trained to stop at the object with a keynote. Seeing the silicon is the habit of moving one layer upstream from whatever is currently famous, and staying there long enough to say what must be physically true — and who, if anyone, captures it.
