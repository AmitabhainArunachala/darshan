# Necessity and Capture

You are at the entrance to Seeing the Silicon. The machine is physical before it is financial: quartz holds molten silicon, resist receives a pattern, and an EUV scanner prints it. This room asks who gets paid for each necessity, why, and what that still does not tell you. The wing's one law is research, never advice.

## 1. Four questions that headlines collapse into one

Take a sentence you will meet often: “Company X is essential to AI.” It sounds like an investment argument. It is only the answer to the first of four separate questions.

**Physical significance** asks what stops working when an input disappears. A quartz crucible can be physically indispensable because molten silicon needs a vessel pure enough not to poison the crystal. This question belongs to engineering and supply-chain mapping.

**Economic capture** asks whether the supplier can keep part of the value its input enables. The usual evidence is not how impressive the product looks. It is the number of qualified alternatives, switching costs, customer bargaining power, capacity discipline, and whether price rises invite new supply. This is the industry-structure question in Michael Mauboussin and Dan Callahan's 22 July 2013 *Measuring the Moat*: value creation and value capture are not the same event.

**Business quality** asks what happens inside one firm. Does it earn attractive returns on the capital it must keep investing? Does it convert accounting profit into cash? Does it depend on one customer, one supplier, or one product generation? A concentrated industry can contain a weak operator. A competitive industry can contain one unusually disciplined operator.

**Investment attractiveness** asks what expectations are already embedded in the price paid for a claim on that business. This is not a timeless property of a company. It changes when the price or the plausible cash-flow path changes. A monopoly can be unattractive if the expectations are too demanding. A merely decent business can be attractive under modest expectations. This room does not answer that question for you because the answer would depend on a current price, your assumptions, and your required return. [Reading the filings](reading-the-filings.html) gives you a repeatable way to expose those assumptions.

Here is the distinction in one line: **technological importance ≠ economic capture ≠ business quality ≠ investment attractiveness.** Keep the four columns separate long enough and many exciting stories become ordinary businesses. A few plain-looking inputs become more interesting.

| Question | Object being assessed | Evidence that can answer it | Evidence that cannot answer it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical significance | A process step or input | Process flow, qualification records, teardown, bill of materials | Market capitalization, management adjectives |
| Economic capture | An industry position | Supplier count, switching and qualification time, gross-margin durability, capacity response | “Critical,” “unique,” or “AI-exposed” by itself |
| Business quality | One operating company | Return on invested capital, cash conversion, customer and supplier concentration, reinvestment need | Industry concentration alone |
| Investment attractiveness | A security at a date and price | Reverse expectations, scenario ranges, dilution, balance sheet, required return | Product importance or past share-price performance |

Edward Chancellor's 2015 account of Marathon Asset Management's capital-cycle work adds the time axis. High returns attract capacity; new capacity weakens returns; weak returns repel capacity; scarcity can return. The right question is therefore not only “is supply concentrated?” It is “what does today's profitability cause suppliers and entrants to build?” The next room, [the capital cycle](the-capital-cycle.html), dates that motion in two real industries.

## 2. Quartz crucibles: necessary, qualified, and still hard to capture

Start at the beginning of a silicon wafer. In the Czochralski process, a seed crystal is slowly pulled from molten polysilicon to grow one cylindrical single crystal. The melt sits in a fused-quartz crucible, a bowl made from very pure silicon dioxide. If the vessel introduces the wrong impurities or fails under heat, the ingot is lost. CoorsTek's current quartz technical page names single-crystal silicon pulling as a critical semiconductor use; Shin-Etsu Chemical's 2024 annual report places quartz crucibles directly between polysilicon and the single-crystal ingot in its manufacturing map.

That settles physical significance. It does not settle capture.

A semiconductor crucible is not interchangeable with kitchen glass. Purity, bubble distribution, thermal behavior, diameter, and the condition of the inner layer matter. A wafer maker qualifies a recipe and supplier because defects propagate into a long, expensive process. That creates some switching friction. But the underlying product is made by several established quartz and wafer-material suppliers. Shin-Etsu supplies crucibles as part of a broad electronics-material portfolio. Ferrotec discloses quartz crucibles beside quartz parts, ceramics, wafers, cleaning, and other equipment-related products. Ningxia Dunyuan Juxin announced an expansion designed for annual output of 100,000 crucibles. No public primary document gives a clean, audited semiconductor-crucible market-share table, so the exact concentration is **UNKNOWN**.

The observable economics still look more commodity-like than the physics sounds. The product is a consumable; customers buy repeatedly. Yet capacity can be added with furnaces, forming equipment, purified feedstock, and qualification time rather than with a decades-long, one-firm technology system. Suppliers themselves announce output expansions in units. Ferrotec's June 2023 presentation aimed to expand quartz-crucible sales sharply from its Yinchuan operation. Its later first-half fiscal 2025 materials reported a rapid fall in photovoltaic crucible demand and lower profitability even as total crucible sales grew. Solar-grade and semiconductor-grade products are not the same market, but the episode shows the supply response embedded in the same manufacturing base: an indispensable vessel can move from shortage to price competition when downstream wafer demand weakens.

So the four answers are:

- Physical significance: high. You do not pull a conventional silicon ingot without a suitable vessel.
- Economic capture: limited to moderate, and grade-specific. Qualification helps; multiple suppliers and expandable capacity restrain it. Exact industry shares are unknown in the public record checked on 2 September 2026.
- Business quality: cannot be inferred from “quartz.” You need segment margins, capital employed, product mix, and customer contracts for the actual supplier.
- Investment attractiveness: unanswered until price and expectations enter. The word “indispensable” has not done that work.

This is the anchor case because it breaks the easiest mistake. A tiny physical cost can sit on the only path to a valuable output and still fail to capture much of that output's economics. The customer may carry the process knowledge. The specification may be tight but reproducible. Capacity may respond. Necessity tells you where failure occurs, not where profit stays.

## 3. Photoresist: the same necessity, a different structure

Photoresist is a light-sensitive chemical film. A fab coats it on a wafer, exposes a pattern through lithography, and develops selected regions so later etch or deposition steps can build the circuit. The [semiconductors](semiconductors.html) room follows the whole fabrication sequence. Here, one point matters: a resist is not just a generic liquid. Its chemistry must work with a wavelength, a process window, the customer's track system, and the yield requirements of a particular layer.

The supplier set is narrow. JSR's 2024 report described a roughly $2 billion total photoresist market and said its argon-fluoride, or ArF, resist share was around 30%. Tokyo Ohka Kogyo's current semiconductor-materials page says TOK had the leading global share by projected 2025 shipment volume and lists both ArF and EUV resists. Shin-Etsu Chemical's 2024 report maps its photoresists and photomask materials into pattern formation. Fujifilm identifies electronic materials, including advanced resist-related products, inside a larger semiconductor-materials portfolio. These company statements establish active qualified suppliers, not a precise four-firm share total. The defensible public description as of 2 September 2026 is a concentrated market led by Japanese firms including JSR, Shin-Etsu, TOK, and Fujifilm, with additional suppliers and different competitive positions by resist generation.

The strongest evidence is what happened when the supply relationship was disturbed. On 1 July 2019, Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry changed licensing procedures for exports to South Korea of resist, hydrogen fluoride, and fluorinated polyimide. The rules took effect on 4 July. This was not a blanket ban; it moved covered shipments from general treatment to individual licensing. A US International Trade Commission staff paper published in 2019 reported that more than 90% of South Korea's 2018 photoresist imports came from Japan and that substituting even a nominally similar formulation required months of process qualification. On 8 August, Japan approved the first covered export; reporting at the time identified it as EUV resist requested by Samsung. In December 2019, Japan allowed bulk licenses for some resist transactions.

Read the episode carefully. South Korean chip production did not stop. Korea diversified and localized parts of the materials chain; later RIETI analysis found that Japanese resist exports did not collapse the way hydrogen-fluoride exports did, partly because bulk permissions returned and sourcing shifted, including toward Belgium. The evidence is therefore not “Japan could turn off Korean fabs at will.” It is narrower and better: governments treated a small-volume chemical as strategically consequential; a customer could not replace it overnight; and qualification plus supplier concentration created real short-run leverage.

That is economic capture's raw material. It still does not guarantee capture for every producer. Formulations turn over. Customers bargain hard and support second sources. Research spending is continuous. JSR's 2024 strategy said it was investing ahead of revenue in EUV resist while defending its ArF position. The supplier must keep earning qualification on the next node.

The four answers now differ from quartz:

- Physical significance: high. Patterning depends on a resist matched to the exposure and process.
- Economic capture: moderate to high in leading formulations. A small qualified supplier group and long requalification support it; the 2019 licensing episode is an observed stress test.
- Business quality: company-specific. A strong formulation franchise may sit inside a diversified chemical group, and research intensity, mix, and allocation determine the actual returns.
- Investment attractiveness: still unanswered. Concentration is an input to a valuation, not its conclusion.

## 4. EUV lithography: one maker, then the harder questions begin

Extreme-ultraviolet lithography, or EUV, prints critical chip layers with 13.5-nanometer light. The machine creates that light from tin plasma, reflects it through multilayer mirrors in a vacuum, and positions wafer and mask with extraordinary precision. [The chip wars](chip-wars.html) explains why this tool also became an export-control chokepoint.

ASML's 2025 annual report states the industry fact plainly: ASML was the world's only manufacturer of EUV lithography systems. It shipped 48 EUV systems during 2025, within 535 total lithography, metrology, and inspection systems reported for the year. The same report identifies Zeiss as the sole supplier of critical EUV optics under an exclusive arrangement and says prolonged loss of that supply would effectively stop ASML's business. Monopoly at one layer can contain single-source dependence at the layer below.

This is the strongest capture setup of the three cases. Customers cannot qualify another EUV scanner maker because there is no other one. The product rests on accumulated engineering, supplier co-development, field service, software, and an installed base. ASML reported 2025 net sales of €32.7 billion and a 52.8% gross margin. Those figures, published with the annual report in early 2026, show substantial accounting capture at the firm rather than merely theoretical bargaining power.

Now resist the final shortcut. Monopoly does not erase cyclicality. Chipmakers place large orders around node ramps and memory cycles. Export licenses can remove markets. A small set of customers has bargaining power and can delay fab plans. ASML's own risk factors say inaccurate demand forecasts and shipment delays can produce insufficient supply or disrupt deliveries. Its capacity depends on specialized suppliers. The machine is physically indispensable at advanced critical layers, but the number of layers, timing of adoption, mix between low-NA and High-NA EUV, service revenue, and customer utilization still govern the business.

Nor does a wonderful business answer the investment question. Public markets can recognize scarcity. Expectations can require many years of growth, margin, and reinvestment before today's owner earns an adequate return. This is how “the market treats” a one-maker technology in analytically useful terms: not as proof that price must rise, but as a demand to separate durable competitive position from the expectations already paid for. A reverse-expectations model starts with those expectations instead of hiding them in an adjective.

## 5. Six layers, four assessments

The same test travels beyond lithography. The entries below are structural assessments as of 2 September 2026, not security recommendations. “Price required” means the fourth column cannot be completed honestly without a dated market price and explicit cash-flow assumptions.

| Stack layer | Physical significance | Economic capture | Business-quality question | Investment attractiveness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quartz crucible | Required for conventional single-crystal silicon pulling | Limited to moderate; qualification helps, but several suppliers and expandable capacity remain | Can the supplier earn through wafer and solar cycles after maintenance and growth capex? | Price and expectations required |
| Advanced photoresist | Required to transfer fine lithographic patterns | Moderate to high in qualified leading formulations; concentrated by supplier and generation | Does research spending renew the qualification franchise faster than old products mature? | Price and expectations required |
| EUV lithography | Required for cost-effective printing of many leading-edge critical layers | High at the scanner layer; one commercial maker as of the 2025 ASML report | Do service, technology leadership, supplier resilience, and returns survive order cycles and controls? | Price and expectations required |
| HBM | Feeds accelerators data at far higher bandwidth than ordinary off-package memory | High during shortage, but three scaled DRAM suppliers and aggressive capacity plans make capture cyclical | Which supplier sustains yield, customer qualification, and mix through the next generation? | Price and expectations required |
| CoWoS packaging | Connects large logic and HBM through an interposer for leading AI systems | High while qualified advanced-packaging capacity is scarce; capacity is expanding | Can the operator scale yield and cash returns without creating its own later surplus? | Price and expectations required |
| Large transformers and data-center power gear | Converts and distributes grid power before compute can consume it | Moderate to high during long lead times; several incumbents are adding capacity | Are margins structural, or the temporary result of backlog, scarcity, and customer urgency? | Price and expectations required |

HBM, or [high-bandwidth memory](the-memory-wall.html), makes the time problem vivid. TrendForce's 31 October 2025 industry note described three major suppliers expanding output, with SK hynix leading through capacity and all three preparing HBM4 production in 2026. Scarcity and qualification can support capture today while the announced supply response weakens it later. [Advanced packaging](advanced-packaging.html) faces the same test around TSMC's CoWoS process. Power gear adds a slower physical cycle: factories, copper, skilled labor, utility specifications, and field qualification stretch the response. [Power and cooling](power-and-cooling.html) follows that chain. [Bottleneck migration](bottleneck-migration.html) asks what happens when yesterday's scarce layer ceases to bind.

## 6. Worked example: read Eaton's “AI data center boom” headline

On Eaton's 2026 news page, one headline says it is expanding Nebraska operations “to meet increasing switchgear demand driven by AI data center boom.” Take that as a current AI-beneficiary claim and run the four questions. Do not begin with the share price.

**Step one: establish physical significance.** Switchgear is the equipment that isolates, protects, and controls electrical circuits. A data center cannot connect megawatts of load safely without breakers, busways, switchboards, transformers, and controls. Eaton's first-quarter 2026 presentation lays out a “grid to chip” architecture and identifies grid limits, power protection, cooling, and modular distribution as deployment constraints. This supports the physical claim. It does not establish which vendor captures the spending.

**Step two: test economic capture.** Ask for observable bargaining evidence. In its 31 July 2026 second-quarter release, Eaton reported that Electrical Americas rolling twelve-month orders grew 41% organically, its backlog was 33% above June 2025, and the electrical businesses had a 1.2 book-to-bill ratio, meaning orders exceeded recognized sales. Electrical Americas segment margin was 27.5%. Those numbers are consistent with strong demand and present capture. They do not tell you how much came from AI, how durable the backlog is, or how competitors and customers will respond.

Now search for the response. The Nebraska headline itself is evidence that supply is expanding. Eaton also completed its acquisition of liquid-cooling supplier Boyd Thermal in March 2026. Competitors and transformer makers have announced their own capacity programs. [The capital cycle](the-capital-cycle.html) tells you why the evidence for capture and the evidence for its eventual erosion can be the same press release.

**Step three: inspect business quality.** Use the filing, not the theme. Eaton's second-quarter release reported company sales of $8.5 billion, up 21% from the second quarter of 2025, with 14% organic growth; company segment margin was 23.1%. Electrical Global sales included a 25% contribution from Boyd Thermal in its first full quarter, so acquisition mix matters. Then read the 2025 10-K for working capital, cash conversion, acquisition accounting, customer mix, raw-material exposure, and cyclicality. The question is whether returns remain attractive after the factories and acquisitions required to meet the boom, not whether quarterly revenue rose.

**Step four: reverse the expectations.** Take the current enterprise value on the date you do the work. Choose a normalized operating margin, tax rate, reinvestment rate, and fade period. Solve for the revenue growth required to make the present value of future free cash flow equal that enterprise value. Do not call the result a target price. It is a translation: “At this price, my model requires roughly X growth for Y years at Z margin.” Then compare X, Y, and Z with the company's capacity, backlog conversion, competitive response, and prior-cycle record. Because market prices change, this room deliberately supplies no current result.

Where did each answer come from? Engineering and the product map answered significance. Orders, backlog, margins, supplier count, and capacity announcements answered capture. The income statement, balance sheet, cash flow, segment disclosures, and risk factors answer quality. Only a dated price plus an explicit model can address attractiveness. One headline opened four document trails. It completed none of them.

## 7. What you can now see

You can now keep a supply-chain map from turning into a portfolio by accident. Quartz showed a necessary input with modest structural capture. Photoresist showed the same necessity plus qualification and concentration, tested by the 2019 Japan–Korea licensing shock. EUV showed one commercial maker and substantial observed capture, then showed why supplier risk, customer cycles, reinvestment, and expectations still matter.

From here, [the capital cycle](the-capital-cycle.html) puts dates on the capacity response. [Reading the filings](reading-the-filings.html) turns management's story into a document trail and a reverse-expectations sketch. [The memory wall](the-memory-wall.html), [advanced packaging](advanced-packaging.html), and [power and cooling](power-and-cooling.html) apply the same discipline to three current bottlenecks. [Physical accounting](physical-accounting.html) keeps the system's energy, water, carbon, and material costs inside the boundary. [Forecasting](forecasting.html) is the place to write the dated signpost that would change one of those assessments.

## Open questions

**Established (FACT):** Fused-quartz crucibles sit in the silicon-ingot process. Advanced photoresists require process qualification and came under Japan's July 2019 export-licensing change for South Korea. ASML identified itself as the only EUV lithography-system manufacturer in its 2025 annual report. Eaton reported strong electrical orders, backlog, and margins for the quarter ended 30 June 2026. None of those facts alone answers all four assessments.

**Contested (HYPOTHESIS):** Current HBM, CoWoS, and power-equipment margins reflect a mixture of durable qualification advantages and temporary shortage. Public segment reporting is too coarse to assign the mixture precisely. Semiconductor-grade quartz crucibles may support better economics than the publicly visible solar-grade price cycle suggests; supplier disclosures do not isolate enough data to settle it.

**Speculation worth holding (WILD):** If announced HBM and advanced-packaging additions move qualified supply above customer demand, then contract terms and lead times should soften by 31 December 2027; the resolving sources are TrendForce's dated contract-price releases and supplier earnings calls. If transformer and switchgear factory additions fail to reduce North American electrical book-to-bill ratios, then scarcity is more persistent than a normal inventory cycle by 31 December 2028; Eaton, GE Vernova, Hitachi Energy, and utility filings provide the resolving record. If a non-ASML supplier ships a production-qualified EUV scanner to a commercial fab, then the one-maker assessment is false by the shipment date; customer acceptance and the supplier's filing are the resolving evidence.

The financial statements train attention in a useful way. The loud object is usually the finished accelerator; the quiet constraints are a chemical film, a white bowl, a transformer ordered years ahead, and the people who qualify each one. Following where the machine stops makes hidden dependence visible. Following who can charge for removing that stop reveals what the dependence is for—and who gets to decide where the next unit of capital and material goes.
