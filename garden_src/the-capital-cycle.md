# The Capital Cycle, Dated

You are one room past the wing's central distinction. [Necessity and capture](necessity-and-capture.html) showed that an input can be essential without keeping much of the value it enables. This room adds motion: attractive returns change supply, but factories arrive late. Dates are the argument.

## 1. Follow the money into the factory

Marathon Asset Management's capital-cycle idea is a plain industrial mechanism. Demand rises faster than supply. Prices, margins, orders, and backlogs improve. Those returns attract capital from incumbents, entrants, lenders, and governments. New capacity takes time to permit, build, equip, qualify, and ramp. When it finally produces, the demand story may have weakened. Price and utilization fall. Capital retreats, projects are delayed, weak operators exit, and the conditions for the next shortage begin.

Edward Chancellor's 2015 *Capital Returns* assembled Marathon's reports around this mechanism. It is not a clock. There is no fixed number of quarters from excitement to glut. It is a chain of evidence you can inspect.

The first useful discipline is to keep five quantities separate:

1. **End demand:** chips, servers, or devices shipped and consumed.
2. **Customer inventory:** products already bought but not yet consumed.
3. **Orders and backlog:** promises moving toward a supplier, sometimes cancellable.
4. **Capital expenditure:** money committed to land, buildings, and equipment.
5. **Qualified output:** usable product leaving the new line at the yield and specification customers require.

A shortage can coexist with falling end demand if customers are still building safety stock. Equipment revenue can rise after memory prices collapse because old backlog is shipping. Announced capacity is not qualified output. These lags are why a dated record beats a thematic story.

**Wafer-fab equipment**, or WFE, means the tools used to form devices on a wafer: deposition, etch, lithography, cleaning, process control, and related systems. **ABF substrate** means a multilayer package substrate built with Ajinomoto Build-up Film, the insulating material that lets fine copper wiring connect a chip package to a circuit board. Both are essential to parts of the [semiconductor](semiconductors.html) chain. Their cycles took different paths because the lead times, market structure, and demand shocks differed.

## 2. Wafer-fab equipment, 2021–2025: one boom, two overlapping cycles

The demand shock is visible in the industry's top line. On 18 March 2022, World Semiconductor Trade Statistics reported that 2021 semiconductor sales reached $555.9 billion, up 26.2% from 2020. Memory sales rose 30.9%; logic rose 30.8%. Fabs facing strong demand and shortages ordered tools.

SEMI's realized equipment data show the response. Global semiconductor-manufacturing equipment sales rose 44% in 2021, from $71.2 billion to $102.6 billion. They rose another 5% in 2022 to $107.6 billion. Those are total-equipment figures, not NAND-only figures.

The order book moved even faster. ASML reported €26.2 billion of net bookings for 2021, more than twice 2020's €11.3 billion, while saying demand exceeded production capacity. It ended 2022 with a €40.4 billion backlog and €30.7 billion of annual bookings. The backlog carried old scarcity into new conditions.

The break appeared first in memory customers' own documents. On 28 September 2022, Micron cut planned fiscal 2023 capital expenditure to about $8 billion, more than 30% below fiscal 2022, and said WFE spending would fall nearly 50%. Lam Research's 19 October 2022 earnings slides described rapid deterioration led by memory and put calendar 2023 WFE more than 20% below a low-$90-billion 2022 base. This was not a vague macro fear. A customer had cut tools; a toolmaker had cut the market.

The cuts deepened. On 21 December 2022, Micron reduced fiscal 2023 capex again to $7.0–7.5 billion from $12.0 billion in fiscal 2022, said WFE spending would fall more than 50%, and reduced DRAM and NAND wafer starts by about 20%. By 28 June 2023, it expected to produce fewer NAND bits in 2023 than in 2022, had cut wafer starts by nearly 30%, and held full-year capex near $7 billion. Applied Materials told analysts on 16 February 2023 that memory customers were deferring NAND and DRAM additions while rebalancing inventory. Lam's 18 October 2023 materials put calendar 2023 WFE in the $80-billion range and named reduced NAND spending and utilization.

Now the important correction. Aggregate equipment did not collapse with NAND. SEMI later reported that total 2023 equipment billings slipped only 1.3%, from $107.6 billion to $106.3 billion. China spending rose 29% while Korea fell 7% and Taiwan fell 27%. SEMI's application forecast put 2023 NAND equipment down 49% to $8.8 billion. So “the WFE cycle” was not one thing. A NAND collapse sat inside a geographically offset total market, while ASML's long backlog and leading-edge logic demand softened the trough.

By 24 January 2024, ASML could call the industry near the bottom while still holding a €39 billion backlog. That sentence is the lag in one receipt. Realized equipment sales then rose 10% to $117.1 billion in 2024 and 15% to $135.1 billion in 2025, according to SEMI releases dated 9 April 2025 and 7 April 2026. The drivers had changed: leading logic, HBM, packaging, test, and China spending replaced part of the earlier broad shortage. Applied's 15 May 2025 call described NAND as an uptick “from very low levels,” not a return to the 2021 order boom.

Here is the full path, with realized data kept apart from company forecasts.

| Cycle stage | Wafer-fab equipment: date and evidence | ABF substrates: date and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Demand shock | 2021: WSTS sales $555.9bn, +26.2%; memory +30.9%, logic +30.8% | 2020–2021: pandemic PC/server demand; TrendForce later dated the peak ABF supply gap to 2021 |
| Orders and scarcity | 2021: ASML bookings €26.2bn; demand above capacity | 2021: Intel reported industry-wide substrate shortages and signed long-term agreements with prepayments |
| Price and margin response | 2021–2022: equipment sales +44% in 2021 and +5% in 2022; supplier backlogs swell | H1 2022: TrendForce reported ABF price, volume, and supplier gross margins rising |
| Capital commitment | 2021–2022: fabs' order boom becomes toolmakers' multi-year production backlog | Apr 2021 onward: Ibiden ¥180bn; Nan Ya, Unimicron, and other lines scheduled for 2022–2024 output |
| Demand and inventory turn | Sep–Dec 2022: Micron cuts FY2023 capex twice and WFE by roughly half | H2 2022: PC/server inventory correction and an Intel platform delay weaken low/mid-layer ABF demand |
| Capacity arrives | 2022–2023: old tool backlog ships even as NAND wafer starts and new orders fall | Q4 2022–Q1 2024: Nan Ya's Shulin/Kunshan phases enter or target mass production; other expansions ramp |
| Glut and retrenchment | 2023: NAND-equipment forecast −49%; total equipment only −1.3% because China offsets it | 2023: utilization falls; Unimicron cuts planned capacity growth; price pressure reaches lower-end substrates |
| Repair or new cycle | 2024–2025: total equipment +10%, then +15%, led by a new mix including HBM and packaging | 2024: Ibiden's package sales and profit remain below the prior peak while high-end AI demand diverges upward |

The table shows why dates and denominators belong together. “NAND WFE fell 49%” and “equipment fell 1.3%” can both be true. One is an application forecast; the other is the realized global total. A narrative that silently swaps them can prove anything.

## 3. ABF substrates, 2020–2024: capacity arrived after the queue moved

Intel's 2021 10-K is a good place to begin because it records the problem from the buyer's side. Intel said industry-wide shortages of substrates and other components hurt several businesses during 2021 and were expected to continue during 2022. It entered long-term supply agreements and made prepayments to secure capacity. TrendForce's 26 April 2023 retrospective places the ABF supply-demand gap's peak in 2021.

Scarcity produced the expected response. TrendForce reported that ABF prices and shipment volume increased in the first half of 2022 and supplier gross margins reached highs. The public release does not provide an auditable monthly ABF price series, so the exact magnitude is **UNKNOWN** outside paid datasets and supplier contracts. The direction is corroborated by what producers built.

Ibiden announced on 27 April 2021 that it would invest ¥180 billion in high-performance IC-package substrates at Gama, with production beginning in fiscal 2023. Nan Ya Plastics' 2022 annual report said Nan Ya PCB's new high-end capacity entered mass production in the fourth quarter of 2022, with Shulin Phase I and Kunshan Phase II aimed at full production in the first quarter of 2023 and Shulin Phase II aimed at mass production in the first quarter of 2024. Unimicron's annual report records NT$32.1 billion of plant and equipment spending in 2022 and NT$22.9 billion in 2023, with NT$24.2 billion planned for 2024 subject to customer and market conditions.

Then the queue moved. From the second half of 2022, PC, notebook, and general-server inventories corrected. Intel delayed a server platform. Counterpoint's 5 July 2023 review said ABF utilization was still falling in the second quarter of 2023 and separated oversupply in low- and mid-end products from firmer high-end AI substrates. TrendForce reported that Unimicron cut planned 2023 capacity growth from about 20% to 3.5%.

The producer accounts show the cost. Ibiden's fiscal 2023 package-substrate sales fell 23.9% to ¥190.7 billion and operating income fell 55.0% to ¥27.3 billion. It cited weak PC and general-server demand and price pressure, while AI-server orders remained strong. In its 31 October 2024 presentation, Ibiden indexed total substrate demand at 100 in calendar 2022 and 82 in 2023; it revised its 2024 view from 95 to 90 and put 2025 in a wide 97–115 range. It also said timing at Gama was under review. The capacity plan had met a segmented market, not the broad shortage for which it was conceived.

This case contains a revealing material layer. Ajinomoto Build-up Film, or ABF, is the insulating film from which the substrate takes its name. Ajinomoto estimated in its 2023 ASV report that it held at least 95% global share as of fiscal 2022. Its 2022 report showed an ABF shipment index rising from 100 in fiscal 2017 to 190 in fiscal 2021, with a company plan of 345 for fiscal 2025. A 2019 analyst briefing described coating as outsourced and the business as relatively asset-light.

That evidence does **not** support the simple line that Ajinomoto “was not the winner.” It supports a narrower conclusion. The material chokepoint did not absorb all the temporary scarcity rent that appeared in substrate prices. Ajinomoto kept a high-share, comparatively asset-light film business; substrate makers spent heavily on plants and then met the utilization and price reset. Public reporting does not isolate Ajinomoto's ABF revenue and profit, so its exact share of the economics is unknown. Here the four-question separation matters again: **technological importance ≠ economic capture ≠ business quality ≠ investment attractiveness.**

## 4. What the two cases teach together

WFE and ABF share a sequence but not a shape.

| Feature | WFE 2021–2025 | ABF 2020–2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Original shock | Broad semiconductor shortage and sales boom | PC, server, and packaging demand exceeded qualified substrate capacity |
| Lead-time buffer | ASML and other tool backlogs carried revenue through the downturn | New substrate plants took roughly two to three years to reach scheduled production |
| First clear turn | Memory customers cut WFE in Sep–Dec 2022 | PC/server inventory correction began in H2 2022 |
| Hidden segmentation | NAND collapsed while China and leading logic supported total equipment | Low/mid-layer ABF weakened while high-layer AI products stayed tighter |
| Capital-cycle loser | Exposed memory-tool demand, not every equipment vendor equally | Capital-heavy substrate capacity aimed at the demand mix that faded |
| Durable chokepoint | Lithography technology and installed-base service softened the cycle; [the chip wars](chip-wars.html) maps why EUV remains a one-maker tool | Ajinomoto's film share persisted, but its segment economics remain undisclosed |

Three habits follow.

First, read customer capex before supplier revenue. Micron's September 2022 cut preceded much of the pain visible in toolmaker results. Second, map when output becomes qualified, not when a board approves a project. Nan Ya's phases make the delay explicit. Third, split the market by application and grade. “Memory,” “equipment,” and “substrates” are often too broad to be useful.

A **book-to-bill ratio** divides orders booked by sales billed in the same period. Above one means the backlog is growing; below one means it is shrinking, assuming comparable definitions and cancellations. It is useful but not sufficient. Customers can double-order in a shortage. Backlog can be non-cancellable or soft. Currency and acquisition mix can distort it. Pair it with utilization, customer inventory, capex, and delivery dates.

## 5. Worked example: HBM and CoWoS, 2024–2026

Now apply the template to today's most visible AI bottlenecks without pretending the cycle has finished.

HBM, taught physically in [the memory wall](the-memory-wall.html), stacks DRAM beside a processor and connects it with dense vertical wiring. CoWoS, taught in [advanced packaging](advanced-packaging.html), is TSMC's family of packaging processes that joins large compute dies and HBM through an interposer and substrate.

**Demand shock and scarcity.** On 25 September 2024, Micron said its HBM output was sold out for calendar 2024 and 2025 with pricing already determined. On 18 April 2024, TSMC said CoWoS capacity would more than double that year and still fall short of demand.

**Price and contracting.** Long customer qualifications and pre-agreed allocations gave suppliers visibility. This is observed scarcity. Do not turn one vendor's sold-out statement into a whole-market utilization number.

**Capital commitment.** On 24 April 2024, SK hynix announced more than ₩20 trillion for its M15X DRAM fab, designed around HBM demand. TSMC said in July 2024 that CoWoS capacity had more than doubled from 2023 to 2024 and that it aimed to double or more again from 2024 to 2025, including outside assembly partners. In December 2025, Micron raised fiscal 2026 capex to about $20 billion. On 18 March 2026 it raised that figure above $25 billion and said HBM and DRAM investment would step up again in fiscal 2027. TSMC raised its 2026 total capex range on 16 July 2026 from $52–56 billion to $60–64 billion; advanced packaging sits inside a broader 10–20% allocation bucket rather than a separately disclosed number.

**Capacity arrival and transition.** SK hynix began HBM3E mass production in March 2024. Micron shipped HBM4 samples in June 2025 for a company-planned 2026 ramp. Each generation requires qualification and yield learning, so nominal wafer capacity is not the same as accepted HBM stacks. TSMC's relative “doubling” disclosures do not provide a common public wafers-per-month denominator across CoWoS versions and partners. Absolute capacity is **UNKNOWN** from the primary documents used here.

Where are we as of the quarter ended 30 June 2026? Write your answer before reading mine. Use only the five stages from Section 1.

My label is **late scarcity with accelerating capacity commitment**. It is an inference, not a reported fact. TSMC still described packaging bottlenecks while raising capex sharply. Micron raised capex twice. SEMI's 14 July 2026 forecast put WFE growth at 23.1% for 2026, with advanced memory and HBM among the drivers. There is ample evidence that capital is entering. There is not yet primary evidence of a broad qualified HBM or CoWoS glut.

The exercise ends by writing one observation that would force you to change the label. Because that statement points forward, it belongs under Open questions with a date and resolving source. [Forecasting](forecasting.html) explains why writing the resolution rule now matters: otherwise you can reinterpret the same backlog after the fact.

## 6. What you can now see

You can now reconstruct a capital cycle from receipts rather than from a price chart. WSTS dates the demand shock. Customer transcripts date the capex cut. Supplier orders and backlog show the lag. Company project announcements tell you when capital was committed. Annual reports and later presentations tell you when capacity arrived and what happened to utilization and profit. [Reading the filings](reading-the-filings.html) is the method for pulling those dates out of a 20-F and a Q&A without accepting the prepared story's order.

You can also see why every broad label needs a denominator. NAND equipment fell hard in 2023 while total equipment barely fell. Low- and mid-layer ABF went into surplus while AI substrates stayed tighter. HBM can be sold out at one qualified supplier while another supplier's product is still waiting for customer acceptance. [Bottleneck migration](bottleneck-migration.html) follows these splits as the constraint moves.

## Open questions

**Established (FACT):** Semiconductor equipment sales rose sharply in 2021 and 2022. Memory customers cut WFE plans in late 2022; 2023 NAND equipment fell far more than aggregate equipment. ABF substrate capacity was committed during the 2021 shortage and some of it arrived into the 2022–2023 inventory correction. HBM and CoWoS suppliers were still committing large amounts of capital through the second quarter of 2026.

**Contested (HYPOTHESIS):** HBM and CoWoS were in late scarcity rather than the opening phase of digestion on 30 June 2026. The label depends on qualified output, cancellations, inventory, and mix that suppliers do not disclose in comparable units. Ajinomoto's ABF franchise captured durable material economics, but public segment reporting cannot show how much of the 2021–2022 substrate scarcity rent it retained.

**Speculation worth holding (WILD):** If TSMC's third-quarter 2026 call says qualified advanced-packaging supply has caught customer demand, or Micron's next earnings materials stop describing HBM as allocated while cutting rather than raising capacity plans, then the cycle label should move from scarcity/buildout toward digestion by 31 October 2026; the resolving sources are TSMC's Q3 2026 transcript and Micron's next prepared remarks and Q&A. If Ibiden's indexed substrate demand exceeds its 2022 base of 100 while package-substrate operating profit remains below fiscal 2022 levels, then mix, price, or new depreciation—not volume alone—remains the binding explanation by 31 March 2027; Ibiden's fiscal 2026 results resolve it. If SEMI's realized 2026 billings materially miss its 14 July 2026 forecast while toolmaker backlogs contract, then capital commitments turned before equipment delivery by 30 April 2027; SEMI's annual billings release and ASML's 2026 annual report resolve it.

The capital cycle is also a cycle of attention. A shortage makes everyone stare at demand just when supply plans become most informative; a glut makes everyone stare at falling prices just when cancellations and exits begin repairing the market. The documents let you move your attention upstream in time. They show what today's returns are causing people to build, and therefore what tomorrow's physical system may have too much—or too little—of.
