Architectures

Ten competing ways to build a utility-scale quantum computer. Each entry carries its scaling hypothesis, its physical dependencies, and the falsifiers that would kill the thesis.

Precision atom qubits in silicon arch-atom-in-silicon

Single dopant atoms placed with atomic precision in isotopically pure silicon; STM-defined fabrication.

Companies: Silicon Quantum Computing

Scaling hypothesis: Atomic-precision placement produces perfectly identical qubits, trading throughput for uniformity.

Dependencies & falsifiers
  • Isotopically enriched silicon (²⁸Si) — ²⁸Si removes nuclear-spin decoherence; enrichment capacity is a shared upstream dependency.
  • Scanning-tunnelling-lithography atomic placement — Precision at the cost of throughput.
  • Dilution refrigeration (sub-100 mK, scaled) — Cross-architecture dependency: nearly every utility-scale design bottoms out in cooling capacity and wiring volume at mK temperatures. Whoever supplies large dilution capacity at acceptable cost sits under most plausible futures.
STM-based fabrication throughput cannot populate utility-scale arrays

Bosonic superconducting qubits arch-bosonic-sc

Error-resilient bosonic encodings in superconducting resonators, trading qubit count for per-qubit protection.

Companies: Nord Quantique

Scaling hypothesis: Hardware-level error suppression reduces the QEC overhead enough that fewer physical modes reach utility.

Dependencies & falsifiers
  • Superconducting-circuit fabrication — Mature relative to alternatives; uniformity and materials losses still bind fidelity.
  • Dilution refrigeration (sub-100 mK, scaled) — Cross-architecture dependency: nearly every utility-scale design bottoms out in cooling capacity and wiring volume at mK temperatures. Whoever supplies large dilution capacity at acceptable cost sits under most plausible futures.
Residual error channels do not suppress far enough to offset the added hardware complexity

[src-darpa-qbi-stageb-2025-11-06]

Neutral-atom arrays arch-neutral-atom

Optically trapped atom arrays with Rydberg-mediated gates; naturally large qubit counts with mid-circuit reload and fidelity questions.

Companies: Atom Computing, Infleqtion (Cold Quanta UK), QuEra Computing

Scaling hypothesis: Optical addressing scales atom counts faster than electrode wiring scales other modalities.

Dependencies & falsifiers
  • Stable laser systems (trapping and gates) — Laser stability and count scale with trap/zone complexity — a shared optical-infrastructure dependency.
  • UHV vacuum packaging — Utility-scale atomic systems need industrial-quality vacuum enclosures, not lab rigs.
  • Room-temperature control electronics — Channel count and synchronization scale with qubit count.
Atom loss and reload overhead dominate as array size grows toward error-corrected operation

[src-darpa-qbi-stageb-2025-11-06]

Optically linked silicon spin qubits arch-optically-linked-spin

Silicon spin qubits entangled through photonic links, combining spin-qubit density with optical networking.

Companies: Photonic Inc.

Scaling hypothesis: Optical interconnect removes the wiring wall that limits monolithic spin arrays.

Dependencies & falsifiers
  • Silicon spin-qubit device fabrication — Shared device substrate between monolithic and optically-linked spin architectures.
  • High-volume silicon-photonics foundry manufacture — Photonic approaches' structural claim: the same industrial base that built telecom optics can build quantum components.
  • Single-photon sources at component scale — Fabrication-tolerant, high-quality sources are the photonic feedstock.
Spin-photon interface efficiency never reaches the entanglement rate the architecture assumes

[src-darpa-qbi-stageb-2025-11-06]

Fusion-based photonic quantum computing arch-photonic-fbqc

Photonic qubits in silicon-photonics chips, entangled into a lattice through two-qubit fusion measurements; utility scale requires millions of near-identical photonic components manufactured in a high-volume fab.

Companies: PsiQuantum, Xanadu

Scaling hypothesis: All hard physics lives in components that a telecom-grade fab can mass-produce; scale becomes a manufacturing and integration problem rather than a coherence problem.

Dependencies & falsifiers
  • High-volume silicon-photonics foundry manufacture — Photonic approaches' structural claim: the same industrial base that built telecom optics can build quantum components.
  • Single-photon sources at component scale — Fabrication-tolerant, high-quality sources are the photonic feedstock.
  • Photon-number-resolving / superconducting single-photon detectors — Detection efficiency and dark count set the loss budget directly.
  • Barium-titanate fast optical switching — Low-loss, high-speed switching is where photonic routing becomes manufacturable or not.
  • Low-loss silicon-nitride waveguides — Waveguide loss is the quiet budget line in every photonic architecture.
  • Low-loss chip-to-fibre coupling — Chip-to-chip interconnect demonstrated at 99.72% conditional fidelity; unconditional performance is part of the loss-budget question.
  • Dilution refrigeration (sub-100 mK, scaled) — Cross-architecture dependency: nearly every utility-scale design bottoms out in cooling capacity and wiring volume at mK temperatures. Whoever supplies large dilution capacity at acceptable cost sits under most plausible futures.
End-to-end photon loss (source → switching → detection) cannot be pushed below the fusion-tolerance threshold at system scale
Optical switching speed or detector efficiency does not reach the fusion network's simultaneous requirements
Cryogenic loading of millions of detectors makes the refrigeration plant impractical

Photonic testbed-class platforms arch-photonic-testbed

Photonic systems built for algorithm demonstration rather than the fusion-lattice utility-scale path — e.g. NQCC's Aegiq and ORCA testbeds.

Companies: Aegiq, ORCA Computing

Scaling hypothesis: Near-term photonic systems test application mapping while utility-scale photonics is built elsewhere.

Dependencies & falsifiers
  • High-volume silicon-photonics foundry manufacture — Photonic approaches' structural claim: the same industrial base that built telecom optics can build quantum components.
  • Single-photon sources at component scale — Fabrication-tolerant, high-quality sources are the photonic feedstock.
Photon losses cap problem size below anything application-relevant

Silicon MOS spin qubits arch-silicon-spin-mos

Electron spins in gate-defined quantum dots at a Si/SiO2 interface, fabricated with industrial 300 mm CMOS processes. The thesis: if qubit structures survive full foundry rules, the semiconductor industry's uniformity, yield and scale become available to quantum processors.

Companies: Diraq, Quantum Motion

Scaling hypothesis: Foundry-fabricated 2D-scalable qubit arrays plus co-integrated cryo-CMOS control reach utility scale without bespoke quantum fabs.

Dependencies & falsifiers
  • 300 mm CMOS foundry processing for quantum devices — The decisive capability for the silicon-spin thesis: quantum-dot devices that survive full industrial design rules.
  • Isotopically enriched silicon (²⁸Si) — ²⁸Si removes nuclear-spin decoherence; enrichment capacity is a shared upstream dependency.
  • Dilution refrigeration (sub-100 mK, scaled) — Cross-architecture dependency: nearly every utility-scale design bottoms out in cooling capacity and wiring volume at mK temperatures. Whoever supplies large dilution capacity at acceptable cost sits under most plausible futures.
  • Cryo-CMOS control electronics — Co-integrated control at deep-cryogenic temperatures is the silicon-spin answer to the wiring wall.
  • Cryogenic wiring and interconnect density — The wiring wall: signal lines into mK space grow faster than qubits unless control moves down-temperature.
Si/SiO2 interface charge noise or valley-splitting variability cannot sustain >99% two-qubit gates at array scale under foundry tolerances
Per-qubit wiring/measurement overhead defeats co-integration before the error-corrected regime is reached
Yield of quantum-dot devices across a full 300 mm wafer stays too low for tiled processors

Modular superconducting processors arch-superconducting-modular

Transmon-class superconducting qubits tiled into modular processors; mature fab and control ecosystem with coherence and crosstalk limits.

Companies: Google Quantum AI, IBM, Rigetti UK

Scaling hypothesis: Modular interconnection of proven processor units plus steady fidelity gains reaches fault tolerance incrementally.

Dependencies & falsifiers
  • Superconducting-circuit fabrication — Mature relative to alternatives; uniformity and materials losses still bind fidelity.
  • Dilution refrigeration (sub-100 mK, scaled) — Cross-architecture dependency: nearly every utility-scale design bottoms out in cooling capacity and wiring volume at mK temperatures. Whoever supplies large dilution capacity at acceptable cost sits under most plausible futures.
  • Room-temperature control electronics — Channel count and synchronization scale with qubit count.
Interconnect loss between modules prevents the modular step from counting as scaling

Topological superconducting qubits arch-topological

Majorana-based topological qubits aiming for hardware-level error protection; the qubit's existence and controllability remain the open question.

Companies: Microsoft

Scaling hypothesis: Topological protection collapses QEC overhead by orders of magnitude if the base qubit works.

Dependencies & falsifiers
  • Superconducting-circuit fabrication — Mature relative to alternatives; uniformity and materials losses still bind fidelity.
  • Dilution refrigeration (sub-100 mK, scaled) — Cross-architecture dependency: nearly every utility-scale design bottoms out in cooling capacity and wiring volume at mK temperatures. Whoever supplies large dilution capacity at acceptable cost sits under most plausible futures.
  • Topological / III-V hybrid material stacks — Materials science is the critical path for topological approaches.
The underlying topological excitation cannot be demonstrated with the braiding fidelity the architecture requires

Trapped-ion (QCCD) processors arch-trapped-ion

Ions held and shuttled in RF traps; state-of-the-art fidelities with slow gates and wiring/packaging pressure as zones multiply.

Companies: IonQ, Oxford Ionics, Quantinuum

Scaling hypothesis: QCCD shuttling and modular photonic networking scale a high-fidelity core into a larger system.

Dependencies & falsifiers
  • Precision ion traps and zone control — Trap fabrication and shuttling control bound trapped-ion scaling.
  • Stable laser systems (trapping and gates) — Laser stability and count scale with trap/zone complexity — a shared optical-infrastructure dependency.
  • UHV vacuum packaging — Utility-scale atomic systems need industrial-quality vacuum enclosures, not lab rigs.
  • Room-temperature control electronics — Channel count and synchronization scale with qubit count.
Gate throughput and shuttling overhead keep useful circuit depth below utility requirements at achievable clock rates
