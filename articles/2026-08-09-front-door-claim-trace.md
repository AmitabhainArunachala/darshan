## Lane: attention_chapel

### [CONFIRMED] 8-second attention span myth: trail dies at Statistic Brain; cited institutions deny the research; myth laundered via a Microsoft Canada marketing report

Verified against the full text of Simon Maybin's BBC More or Less piece 'Busting the attention span myth' (Feb 2017), retrieved directly. Exact chain confirmed: (a) 'All those references lead back to a 2015 report by the Consumer Insights team of Microsoft Canada, who surveyed 2,000 Canadians and also studied the brain activity of 112 people'; (b) 'the figure that everyone picked up on... did not actually come from Microsoft's research. It appears in the report, but with a citation for another source called Statistic Brain' — so 'laundered via a Microsoft Canada marketing report' is accurate; (c) Statistic Brain's own sources are 'infuriatingly vague' and 'My attempts to contact Statistic Brain came to nothing too' — the trail genuinely dies there. ONE WORD TO TIGHTEN: 'cited institutions deny the research' slightly overstates. BBC's exact words: 'when I contact the listed sources - the National Center for Biotechnology Information at the US National Library of Medicine, and the Associated Press - neither can find any record of research that backs up the stats.' They did not issue denials; they found no record. Suggested recast: 'the sources Statistic Brain cited — the US National Library of Medicine's NCBI and the Associated Press — told the BBC they could find no record of any such research.' If quoting the myth's numbers: 12 seconds in 2000 falling to 8, vs a goldfish's supposed 9.

Source (SECONDARY (the BBC investigation is itself the original trail-tracing)): https://www.bbc.com/news/health-38896790

### [CONFIRMED] Goldfish actually have months-long memory and are used in labs as a model organism for memory research

Two independent expert sources confirm both halves. BBC (same Maybin piece): Prof Felicity Huntingford (fish behaviour, ~50 years) — 'Goldfish can perform all the kinds of learning that have been described for mammals and birds' and 'they've become a model system for studying the process of learning and the process of memory formation'; hundreds of papers, fish-memory study as early as 1908. Live Science (interviewing Culum Brown, fish cognition, Macquarie University): 'Goldfish memories can actually last for weeks, months and even years'; goldfish are 'a common model for studying memory and learning in fishes'; known since the 1950s-60s; they remember maze/escape tasks 'weeks, and even months, later'. Caveat for merciless precision: Brown also says 'It is hard to pinpoint an exact time span for goldfish memory' — so write 'memories lasting months' or 'weeks to months, in some studies years', not a single fixed number. 'Months-long memory (lab use)' as claimed is safe as written.

Source (SECONDARY): https://www.livescience.com/goldfish-memory.html

### [NEEDS_RECAST] Gloria Mark's screen-attention measurements: ~2.5 minutes on one screen circa 2004 falling to a median ~40 seconds in recent studies

Every individual figure is confirmed from Mark's own words in the APA Speaking of Psychology transcript (Feb 2023): 2004 — 'we found the average attention span on any screen to be two and a half minutes on average' (measured with stopwatches); 'around 2012 we found it to be 75 seconds' (computer logging); last 5-6 years — 'we found it to average about 47 seconds, and others have replicated this result within a few seconds'; 'The median is 40 seconds... half of all the measurements that we found were 40 seconds or less.' THE PROBLEM: the claim as worded compares a 2004 MEAN (2.5 min) to a recent MEDIAN (40 s) — a hostile fact-checker will call that mixing statistics to inflate the decline. Suggested recast: 'In 2004, Gloria Mark's team clocked an average of about 2.5 minutes on any one screen before switching; by the late 2010s the average had fallen to about 47 seconds — with a median of just 40.' Alternatively mean-to-mean only: '...had fallen to roughly 47 seconds.' Also say 'average attention on a screen before switching', not 'attention span' generically — that is what she measured. Note 'recent studies' = her studies roughly 2016-2021, replicated by others, as stated in the 2023 interview.

Source (PRIMARY): https://www.apa.org/news/podcasts/speaking-of-psychology/attention-spans

### [NEEDS_RECAST] Two-thirds of people who confessed to an AI hologram of Jesus in a Swiss chapel reported a spiritual experience (Deus in Machina, St. Peter's Chapel Lucerne 2024)

Core statistic is real but three words in the framing are contradicted by the primary record (Guardian, 21 Nov 2024, full text retrieved; plus Hochschule Luzern Immersive Realities project page). (1) 'confessed' is WRONG: theologian Marco Schmid on the record — 'It's not a confession. We are not intending to imitate a confession.' The avatar sat in the confessional booth only because the church was 'short on space and seeking a place where people could have private conversations.' Visitors 'were invited to pose questions.' (2) 'hologram' is WRONG: it was 'a long-haired image of Jesus beamed through a latticework screen' — an AI avatar on a computer setup, not a hologram (some aggregators say hologram; the Guardian and HSLU do not). (3) 'two-thirds of people who [used it]' has the wrong denominator: more than 1,000 people interacted over the two-month run (23 Aug - 20 Oct 2024, per HSLU); the two-thirds figure comes from voluntary feedback: 'feedback from more than 230 users suggested two-thirds of them had found it to be a spiritual experience, said Schmid.' So it was self-selected feedback from ~23% of users, not a survey of all participants. Suggested recast: 'Over two months in 2024, more than 1,000 people sat in the confessional booth of St Peter's Chapel in Lucerne and talked with an AI avatar of Jesus — an experiment its organizers were careful to say was not confession. Of the 230-plus visitors who left feedback, two-thirds described the exchange as a spiritual experience.' Also correct: 'Deus in Machina' name, Peterskapelle = oldest church in Lucerne, 100+ languages, built with the Immersive Realities Center at Lucerne University of Applied Sciences (HSLU). Secondary source: https://sites.hslu.ch/immersive-realities/en/deus-in-machina-ai-jesus-english/

Source (SECONDARY): https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2024/nov/21/deus-in-machina-swiss-church-installs-ai-powered-jesus

### [NEEDS_RECAST] Three-quarters of one country's civilian casualties now come from drones

NOT supported as a drones-only figure — the 75% appears to be built by conflating OHCHR categories. Verified OHCHR data: June 2026 (worst month for Ukraine's civilians since Feb 2022): long-range weapons — which OHCHR defines as 'powerful missiles with area-wide effects AS WELL AS drones', i.e. missiles included — caused 45% of casualties; short-range drones caused 89 killed + 588 injured of 293 killed + 1,990 injured, i.e. ~30%. Those sum to ~75% only if you count every missile as a drone. Drones-only shares OHCHR actually supports: short-range drones = ~30% countrywide (H1 2026: 418 killed + 2,437 injured of 1,396 + 7,978), and in March 2026 short-range drones killed 66 of 211 civilians — 'more than any other weapon.' OHCHR publishes no missile-vs-drone split inside the long-range category, so no primary source yields 'three-quarters from drones.' Exact recast: 'In June 2026 — the worst month for Ukraine's civilians since the full-scale invasion began — roughly three-quarters of the casualties came from missiles and drones: 45 per cent from long-range strikes on cities, another 30 per cent from the short-range drones that now kill more civilians near the frontline than any other weapon.' Alternative if the sentence must stay drone-pure: 'Short-range attack drones are now the single deadliest weapon for one country's civilians — in March 2026 they killed more people in Ukraine than any other weapon class.'

Source (PRIMARY): https://ukraine.ohchr.org/en/Protection-of-Civilians-in-Armed-Conflict-June-2026

### [NEEDS_RECAST] In the jammed zones the final approach is flown by no one

Directionally TRUE and well documented, but overstated as universal. Documented: Kyiv Post (Sept 2025) — two Ukrainian firms began MASS PRODUCTION of FPV strike drones with the TFL-1 module giving autonomous 'fire-and-forget' terminal guidance for the final 400–500 m (~$448/unit, marginally above a classic FPV); Defense Express explains the machine-vision homing is immune to jamming precisely because it no longer relies on the operator link; Forbes (Jan 2026) covers the scaling. However: (a) the operator still flies the drone TO the lock-on point, so only the terminal leg is unmanned; (b) the other mass counter to jamming is fiber-optic drones, which a human flies the entire way — so 'the final approach is flown by no one' is false for a large share of jammed-zone strikes. Exact recast: 'and in the jammed zones the final approach is increasingly flown by no one — machine-vision terminal-guidance modules are in mass production on both sides precisely because jamming severs the human link in the last few hundred meters.'

Source (SECONDARY): https://www.kyivpost.com/post/60152

### [WRONG] Legislatures are writing nothing into law (re AI consciousness/welfare)

False as written — legislatures are actively writing AI legal status into law; what they are writing is denial. Enacted statutes: Idaho HB 720 (2022) — 'artificial intelligence, nonhuman animals, and inanimate objects shall not be granted personhood'; Utah HB 249 (2024) — bars government entities from granting legal personhood to AI (plus animals, bodies of water, etc.), and its enumerated list has become model text copied by other states; North Dakota HB 1361 (signed 12 Apr 2025 — amended into a general statutory definition of 'person' rather than an outright prohibition, a nuance worth respecting if ND is named). Oklahoma's HB 3546 passed its House 94–2 in March 2026 (full enactment not verified — say 'passed the House', not 'law'). Per Smith, Caviola & Alexander (SSRN) and The Regulatory Review (June 2026): 23 such 'exclusion bills' across 12 states since 2022, with Missouri's 'AI Non-Sentience and Responsibility Act', Ohio HB 469, Tennessee, South Carolina and Washington pending. Exact recast: 'Legislatures ARE writing law — but only one kind: Idaho, Utah, and North Dakota have already put into statute that an AI cannot be a person, and Oklahoma's House passed its version 94–2. What no legislature anywhere has written is a single protective standard — the laws all say what AI is not, and none says what, if anything, might be owed.' That recast is stronger for the piece's argument and fully sourced.

Source (SECONDARY): https://www.theregreview.org/2026/06/29/rost-legislating-ai-consciousness-without-an-exit/

### [CONFIRMED] The serious estimates refuse to say zero (AI moral-patienthood probability)

Fairly represents the published literature. 'Taking AI Welfare Seriously' (Long, Sebo, Butlin, Finlinson, Fish, Harding, Pfau, Sims, Birch, Chalmers — arXiv 2411.00986, Nov 2024) argues there is 'a realistic possibility' that some near-future AI systems will be conscious and/or robustly agentic, explicitly refusing both zero and certainty. The Dreksler et al. survey ('Subjective Experience in AI Systems: What Do AI Researchers and the Public Believe?', arXiv 2506.11945, 2025; 582 published AI researchers + 838 representative US public) found a MEDIAN researcher estimate of 25% probability of AI systems with subjective experience by 2034 (public median: 30%). Anthropic's Kyle Fish has publicly put consciousness-related properties in current models at roughly 15–20%. If the piece wants a concrete anchor, exact wording: 'when 582 published AI researchers were surveyed, their median estimate of the chance that AI systems with subjective experience exist by 2034 was twenty-five per cent — not zero.'

Source (PRIMARY): https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986

Note the caveat: Anthropic HAS publicly bought carbon removal and made electricity-price commitments — those are actions, not footprint disclosure, so the claim survives if worded as disclosure specifically.

The pattern is REAL and well documented, but the plural 'earnings calls' rests on ONE verified earnings-call case. Both halves confirmed: (1) Announced in AI's name — Amazon's Oct 28, 2025 layoff memo from SVP Beth Galetti (https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-workforce-reduction) framed 14,000 cuts with 'This generation of AI is the most transformative technology we've seen since the Internet'; Salesforce's Benioff said AI let him cut ~4,000 support roles ('I need less heads,' Fortune, Sept 2025, https://fortune.com/2025/09/02/salesforce-ceo-billionaire-marc-benioff-ai-agents-jobs-layoffs-customer-service-sales/); Klarna cut ~700 citing AI then rehired after quality fell; TechCrunch maintains a running list of 20+ tech companies citing AI in 2026 layoffs (https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/25/the-running-list-major-tech-layoffs-in-2026-where-employers-cited-ai/). (2) The walk-back — on Amazon's Q3 2025 earnings call (Oct 30, 2025), two days after the memo, CEO Andy Jassy told analysts the cuts were 'not really financially driven, and it's not even really AI-driven, not right now at least… it's culture' (GeekWire; CNN https://www.cnn.com/2025/10/30/tech/amazon-layoffs-andy-jassy-ai-culture; Axios https://www.axios.com/2025/10/31/amazon-layoffs-ai-andy-jassy). Supporting the broader pattern: a Resume.org survey found ~6 in 10 companies admit framing layoffs/hiring slowdowns as AI-driven when the real reason is financial; Sam Altman himself called it 'AI washing'; MIT economist David Autor calls AI-attribution a long-running cover story (Fortune, May 2026, https://fortune.com/2026/05/31/tech-companies-ai-washing-layoffs-wix-block-snap-atlassian-disposable-workers/; CNBC https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/19/firms-are-blaming-ai-for-job-cuts-critics-say-its-a-good-excuse.html). But Klarna's admission came in Bloomberg interviews, not an earnings call — Amazon is the only clean on-the-call case found. SUGGESTED RECAST: 'Layoffs are announced in AI's name — more than twenty tech companies cited AI in 2026 cuts alone — while Amazon's CEO, two days after a layoff memo invoking AI as the most transformative technology since the Internet, told analysts on an earnings call that the cuts were "not even really AI-driven… it's culture."' Alternatively drop the earnings-call specificity: '…while executives, pressed, admit AI isn't the reason.' As written, a fact-checker asking "which executives, which calls?" finds exactly one.

Source (SECONDARY): https://www.geekwire.com/2025/its-culture-amazon-ceo-says-massive-corporate-layoffs-were-about-agility-not-ai-or-cost-cutting/

### [NEEDS_RECAST] More words are produced every day than in whole past centuries

Directionally defensible, but no auditable estimate exists for this exact comparison — as a bare assertion it invites the Eric-Schmidt treatment (his 2010 'every two days we create as much information as from the dawn of civilization to 2003' line was widely debunked, and this sentence reads as the same genre). What IS checkable: Sam Altman's Feb 9, 2024 post (primary, verified): 'openai now generates about 100 billion words per day. all people on earth generate about 100 trillion words per day' (https://x.com/sama/status/1756089361609981993). Loose supporting arithmetic: Google's 2010 count of ~130M distinct books ever published × ~75k words ≈ ~10 trillion words in every book ever printed — less than one day of humanity's output on Altman's own figure, and ChatGPT's 100B words/day alone plausibly exceeds the distinct printed text of an entire early-modern century (~350k 16th-century editions × ~80k words ≈ ~28B words). So the direction survives even harsh discounting — but every input is an informal estimate, the strongest one a CEO's tweet, and 'produced' (spoken? written? distinct? copies?) is undefined. SUGGESTED RECAST, two safe registers: (a) Anchored: 'By OpenAI's own arithmetic, its models alone were generating some 100 billion words a day by early 2024 — machine text on a scale where a single day plausibly outweighs whole centuries of print.' (b) Explicitly rhetorical: 'More words are produced in a day, by any loose count anyone has dared to publish, than whole past centuries managed — and the fact that no one can audit the number is part of the point.' Do NOT leave it as an unattributed statement of fact.

Source (PRIMARY): https://x.com/sama/status/1756089361609981993
