# AI Compute & Energy — $40,000 Quick Portfolio Snapshot

**As of:** 2026-09-16  
**Project lens:** Compute Ecology Intelligence / Darshan  
**Status:** point-in-time research artifact; not a standing recommendation  

## Purpose

Freeze a dated view of how a hypothetical USD 40,000 portfolio could express the Compute Ecology thesis: AI compute is constrained by a physical dependency chain spanning advanced fabrication and packaging, accelerators/custom silicon, networking and optical interconnect, lasers/photonics, electrical infrastructure, generation, cooling, and ecological constraints.

Core question: **as AI scales, which physical constraint becomes harder to substitute, expands faster than supply, and therefore has a plausible mechanism for capturing economic value?**

Technical importance, scarcity, revenue growth, durable margins, valuation, and investment return are separate propositions and must be tested separately.

### Why this version is more aggressive

52.5% is concentrated in CRDO/LITE/MRVL, expressing a stronger hypothesis that the marginal AI infrastructure bottleneck migrates toward connectivity, lasers/optics and custom interconnect rather than remaining concentrated in accelerator scarcity.

The absence of NVIDIA is intentional in this experimental portfolio, not a negative view on NVIDIA. The purpose is to investigate less-obvious beneficiaries of the next marginal dollar of AI infrastructure spending.

### Physical dependency map

Compute / custom silicon  
→ advanced fabrication + packaging  
→ high-speed networking  
→ electrical-to-optical conversion  
→ lasers / III-V / modulators / waveguides  
→ coupling, packaging, alignment and optical test  
→ rack power delivery  
→ protection / conversion / switchgear  
→ cooling and heat rejection  
→ generation + grid connection  
→ water, materials, land, emissions and other ecological constraints

A bottleneck removed at one layer can migrate to another layer rather than disappear.

## Portfolio discipline

Do not sell merely because price falls. Re-underwrite when the physical or economic thesis changes. Examples:

- If 1.6T optics rapidly commoditize, re-underwrite CRDO/LITE.
- If integrated photonics structurally removes value from discrete laser suppliers, re-underwrite LITE.
- If hyperscalers vertically integrate connectivity and capture supplier economics, re-underwrite MRVL/CRDO.
- If grid interconnection becomes substantially more constraining than cooling, examine migration toward power/grid suppliers.
- If generation/grid capacity begins expanding faster than credible compute demand, re-underwrite GEV and energy exposure.
- If a technically critical supplier lacks pricing power because of substitutability or customer concentration, do not confuse importance with value capture.

## Deployment concept recorded on 2026-09-16

For the aggressive portfolio, a staged entry was preferred conceptually: approximately $15k initially, $10k over the following 4–6 weeks, $10k over the subsequent 2–3 months, and $5k retained tactically until another earnings/industry evidence cycle, ultimately converging toward the target weights rather than treating today's allocation as immutable.

## Next experiment: three-risk portfolio comparison

Generate three independently researched USD 40,000 portfolios from the same as-of date:

1. **Level I — Durable / lower-risk AI infrastructure:** larger profitable platforms and diversified infrastructure; meaningful reserve; lower single-name concentration.
2. **Level II — Concentrated Compute Ecology:** stronger exposure to networking, optics, packaging, power and thermal bottlenecks while retaining anchors.
3. **Level III — Aggressive / asymmetric:** materially greater exposure to smaller or higher-beta suppliers where evidence supports a plausible bottleneck/value-capture mechanism, while avoiding binary junk, leverage and unsupported moonshots.

Each portfolio must total exactly USD 40,000 and 100%, and must be evaluated against the same evidence rubric so differences reflect risk appetite rather than inconsistent research.

## Research rubric for future snapshots

For every candidate, capture:

- market cap and valuation metrics as of the stated date;
- revenue growth, gross/operating margin and free cash flow;
- AI/data-center revenue exposure where actually disclosed;
- customer concentration;
- position in the physical dependency graph;
- substitutability and qualification barriers;
- manufacturing yield/capacity constraints where relevant;
- pricing power and plausible mechanism of value capture;
- capital intensity;
- major competitors and hyperscaler vertical-integration risk;
- ecological/resource dependencies;
- thesis, strongest counter-thesis, falsifier and next observable milestone;
- stage: research/demo, qualification, ramp, volume production, or deployed infrastructure.

Never infer investment quality from technical excitement alone. Never infer ecological benefit from component efficiency alone. Preserve publication date versus event date and distinguish company claims from independently verified deployment evidence.
